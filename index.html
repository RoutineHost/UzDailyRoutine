Daily nutrition guide

Use this as a flexible pattern, not a strict diet. Portions should match hunger, activity and appetite.

Build a satisfying main meal
½ plate vegetables, ¼ protein (dal, chana, rajma, eggs, paneer, curd, fish or chicken), and ¼ rice/roti/millets. Add a little healthy fat.
Support iron intake
Include dal, beans, chickpeas, leafy greens, sesame, eggs or meat if eaten. Pair plant iron with vitamin-C foods she tolerates, such as cooked capsicum, tomato or guava. Amla and sour drinks are not required.
Hair and skin nutrition
Prioritise adequate protein, iron, zinc, omega-3 sources, colourful vegetables and enough total food. No food or drink can guarantee acne-free skin or hair regrowth.
Hydration and caffeine
Keep water nearby and drink regularly. If using tea/coffee, keep it earlier in her waking period and avoid it within about 6–8 hours of planned sleep if it affects rest.

Night-shift timing: how to use this plan

Use these approximate India-time slots as a starting point. Shift them slightly if her work or appetite varies.

3:00 PM

Wake up + hydrate

Have a glass of water. Freshen up and give herself a few minutes to wake fully.

3:15 PM

Pre-workout snack

1 medium banana + water. If she enjoys juice, add a small 150 ml glass of fresh, unsweetened juice; whole fruit is usually more filling. No energy drink or added sugar needed.

3:45 PM

Workout of the day

Start the planned session after the snack settles. If she feels hungry or weak, add a little curd or toast and wait longer. On recovery/rest days, take an easy walk or stretch instead.

4:30–5:00 PM

Post-workout meal

Have a proper meal with protein and carbohydrates: for example eggs/paneer/tofu or dal, with roti/rice and vegetables. Do not rely on juice alone after exercise.

7:00 PM

Meal before work

Eat a balanced main meal before the 8 PM shift: rice/roti, dal/beans or another protein, vegetables and curd.

11:00 PM

Shift snack

Fruit with roasted chana or nuts, or plain curd/yoghurt. Keep water nearby.

2:00–3:00 AM

Meal during shift

Pack a complete meal such as khichdi with dal and curd, or roti with paneer/egg/chicken and vegetables.

6:30–7:00 AM

After-shift light snack, if hungry

Milk/fortified soy milk, curd or a small bowl of oats. Wind down and aim to sleep around 8 AM.

Health notes worth acting on

Important: Periods that have become unusually light (1–2 days), fatigue/sleepiness, acne and hair thinning together deserve a medical assessment. Please arrange a gynaecologist or primary-care appointment rather than assuming it is a hormone imbalance. A clinician may consider pregnancy testing if relevant, thyroid testing, CBC/ferritin and other tests based on her history. Do not stop or change any medication without speaking to the prescriber.

Homeopathic products are not proven to regulate periods or treat the causes of hair thinning/acne. Tell her clinician exactly what she is taking, including the product name and ingredients.

Seek urgent care for very heavy bleeding (soaking a pad hourly for 2 hours), fainting, severe pelvic pain, chest pain or shortness of breath.
